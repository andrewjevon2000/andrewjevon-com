#UX Case Study · Content Architecture

677 pages, one clear answer each

Cleaning up years of overlapping content for an identity-verification company in two markets.

#PeriodOct 2025 – now
#RoleFreelance SEO & content IA
#MarketIndonesia + Philippines
#Result9% → 32% qualified leads

ProblemHundreds of pages answered the same questions in slightly different ways, so readers landed on outdated answers and nothing ranked well.
ApproachInventory every URL, give each topic one canonical page per market, and build a toolkit so the team keeps it clean.
#01 Context

When a site competes with itself

Verihubs sells identity verification to banks, fintechs, and other regulated businesses. Its buyers research carefully: compliance teams, product managers, and engineers reading about e-KYC, liveness detection, and regulation before they ever talk to sales.

Years of content production had left many pages covering the same topic. For a reader, that means landing on an older, thinner version of the answer. For search engines, it means no page is strong enough to rank.

Design goal

Make sure that every question a buyer asks has one clear, current, trustworthy answer, in the right language for their market, and that the team can keep it that way without hours of manual checks.

#02 Content Audit

A content inventory of 677 URLs

A content inventory is a classic UX method: list every page, what it's for, and how it performs. Here it was done at scale with Search Console, Ahrefs, and Screaming Frog, looking for pages that target the same question (keyword cannibalization).

#03 Two Markets

The right page for the right country

Verihubs serves Indonesia and the Philippines. Without the right signals, a buyer in Manila can land on the Indonesian page, with the wrong regulation, language, and contact details. Hreflang tells search engines which version belongs to which market, and JSON-LD schema describes the company and its products clearly.

#Protecting trust

Two negative-SEO attack clusters, identified

A backlink-gap analysis surfaced two clusters of suspicious links pointing at the site. For an identity-verification brand, search reputation is part of the product's trust, so spotting them was part of keeping the experience safe.

#04 Designing for the team

An AI-assisted toolkit for the people who maintain it

A clean structure decays if nobody can maintain it. The team's internal users needed less manual reporting and a safety net before publishing. I designed a Claude-assisted SEO ops toolkit around their actual workflow.

Before

  • Manual Search Console reports, rebuilt every cycle
  • Audits done ad hoc, when something broke
  • New articles published without checking overlap or compliance

After

  • Automated GSC reporting
  • Repeatable audits on a documented process
  • Pre-publish sweep: overlap and compliance check before anything goes live

#05 Key Design Decisions

Three choices that shaped the system

01

One question, one page

Instead of publishing more content, consolidate. Each topic gets one canonical page that's complete and current.

WhyReaders deserve the best answer, not the oldest one. Search engines reward the same thing.
Signal677 URLs mapped into a consolidation plan.
02

Market first, then language

Pages are structured per market (Indonesia, Philippines), not only translated, so regulation and contact routes match the reader.

WhyFor a compliance product, the wrong country's regulation is worse than no answer.
SignalHreflang + JSON-LD fixes for both markets.
03

Design for the maintainer, not just the visitor

The pre-publish sweep catches overlap before it happens, so the structure stays clean as the team keeps writing.

WhyCannibalization came from years of unchecked publishing. Fix the cause, not just the symptom.
SignalThe process now runs as a repeatable, documented system.

#07 What I learned
"More content isn't more help. One great answer beats four good ones."

A content audit is UX research. Mapping every page shows exactly where readers get confused, long before any analytics dashboard does.

The maintainer is a user too. Designing the team's workflow is what keeps the structure clean after the project ends.

Next time: I'd pair the audit with a short survey of the sales team to learn which questions prospects ask most on calls, then check that each one has a page.
